<h2><a href="https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/problems/change-all-even-bits-in-a-number-to-03253/1">Unset All Even Bits</a></h2><h3>Difficulty Level : Difficulty: Basic</h3><hr><div class="problems_problem_content__Xm_eO" style="--text-color: var(--problem-text-color);"><p><span style="font-size: 18.6667px;">Given a non-negative integer <strong>n</strong>, update its binary representation by changing all bits at <strong>even </strong>indices to <strong>0</strong> while leaving all bits at odd indices unchanged.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-size: 18.6667px;"><strong>Note:</strong> Indices are <strong>0-based</strong>, counted from the rightmost bit. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-size: 18px;"><strong>Examples :</strong></span></p>
<pre><span style="font-size: 18px;"><strong>Input: </strong>n = 30
<strong>Output:&nbsp;</strong>10
<strong>Explanation</strong>: </span><span style="font-size: 18px;">The binary representation of 30 is <strong>1</strong>1<strong>1</strong>1<strong>0</strong>. Considering 0-based indices from the right, the bits at even indices (0, 2, and 4) are set to 0, resulting in <strong>0</strong>1<strong>0</strong>1<strong>0</strong>. The decimal value of 01010 is 10.</span></pre>
<pre><span style="font-size: 18px;"><strong>Input</strong>: n = 10<br><strong>Output:</strong>&nbsp;10&nbsp;
<strong>Explanation</strong>: </span><span style="font-size: 18px;">The binary representation of 10 is 1<strong>0</strong>1<strong>0</strong>. Considering 0-based indices from the right, the bits at even indices (0 and 2) are already 0. Therefore, the binary representation remains 1010, and the resulting value is 10.</span></pre>
